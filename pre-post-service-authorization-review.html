Operating principle

The authorization workflow begins before the request is submitted.

If the requirement is never identified, the downstream team may inherit a much harder exception after the service or claim already exists. PARCS now starts with the question: Did the authorization or referral requirement become visible early enough for someone to act on it?

Patient-to-professional perspective

The patient experiences the downstream consequence.

“Why didn't anyone tell me this needed approval?”
“Why is insurance denying this after I already had the service?”
“Why am I being asked for information now?”

Healthcare operations has to determine whether the authorization requirement became visible early enough for someone to act on it.

Where did the workflow first lose control? Maybe not at the denial. Maybe not at submission. The first control failure may have happened when the requirement should have been identified.

Career simulation boundary

What PARCS does not decide

PARCS does not determine medical necessity, coverage, coding accuracy, payer liability, contractual rights, legal obligations, or whether a real payer must permit retroactive or retrospective authorization. It does not substitute for current payer/program guidance or qualified professional review.
