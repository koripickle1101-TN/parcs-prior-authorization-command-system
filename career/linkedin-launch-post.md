I built PARCS 2.0 to explore a healthcare operations question I keep coming back to:

What if prior authorization problems are not just task delays, but workflow reliability failures?

PARCS 2.0 is a portfolio project focused on prior authorization reliability, patient access risk, denial prevention, documentation readiness, trust failure events, reconciliation density, and clean claim readiness.

The system looks at questions like:

- Which authorization cases are most likely to break?
- Why are they at risk?
- Who owns the next action?
- What downstream damage could happen if the issue is not resolved?
- What metric proves the workflow improved?

I designed the project with synthetic data only. No patient information, protected health information, or real payer case data is used.

This project demonstrates workflow analysis, revenue cycle risk thinking, KPI design, operational reporting, prior authorization process design, and patient access risk detection.
