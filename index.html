PARCS 2.0 Case Study

A prior authorization reliability system designed to expose workflow risk before it becomes patient access disruption, denial risk, or staff burden.

Problem

Prior authorization delays are rarely isolated task failures.

They often come from weak workflow visibility, unclear ownership, payer variation, documentation gaps, late escalation, and hidden manual reconciliation.

System Designed

PARCS 2.0 turns the workflow into a reliability-control model.

The system uses risk scoring, trust failure logging, documentation readiness checks, payer rule intelligence, reconciliation tracking, and clean claim readiness review.

Stress Tests

Three failure modes were tested.

Trust Collapse, Operational Drift, and Human Dependency were used to evaluate whether the system can remain traceable, routable, measurable, and recoverable under pressure.

Before and After

4.2/10 → 7.7/10

The original workflow showed critical vulnerability. PARCS 2.0 improved the model into an operationally viable system with moderate residual risk.

Skills Demonstrated

Healthcare workflow analysis, revenue cycle improvement, prior authorization reliability, patient access risk detection, denial prevention logic, KPI design, operational telemetry, root-cause thinking, and executive reporting.

Executive insight: Authorization approval is not the finish line. Clean claim readiness is the finish line.
